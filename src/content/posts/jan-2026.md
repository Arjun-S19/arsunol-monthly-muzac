<h2 align="center">week 1 – jan 4th to jan 10th: focus on enka</h2>

this week i chose to start off with the enka genre as a whole, as i noticed from some previous listening that it has relatively the same musical tendencies, just adapted to mix with the popular styles of that time as well. enka stems from a line of evolving japanese genres:

![](https://mermaid.ink/img/Zmxvd2NoYXJ0IExSCiAgICBzaG9teW8gLS0+IGthdGFyaW1vbm8gLS0+IG1pbnlvWyJtaW7igJl5byJdIC0tPiByeXVrb2thIC0tPiBlbmth)

- **shomyo:** buddhist chanting

- **katarimono:** storytelling in a musical form with a biwa, and later a shamisen  
    - both are lute instruments, where biwa is the predecessor of the shamisen

- **min’yo:** folk songs about daily life

- **ryukoka:** proto-enka that emerged in the 1910s/1920s where min’yo and japanese scales were combined with western music styles and instrumentation  
    - foundation for a lot of modern japanese genres

- **enka:** the good ol’ kobushi vocal ballads that we all know and love

the general topics of enka music are love, loneliness, and nostalgia, so combined with the kobushi vocal style that stresses syllables, it creates a ballad that’s deep and meaningful. i notice that while the instrumentation tends to vary by decade and artist, the scales and melody progressions tend to be more traditional. instrumentation can be like [rock](https://open.spotify.com/track/4R9umA1BrylAFA8zuw1RoZ?si=61b5e99243e64db5) or [jazz-pop](https://open.spotify.com/track/3NZOGNjhuaybJknV7H8k77?si=66f1756b5ebb4595) or [traditional japanese](https://open.spotify.com/track/1HJriOBdhA5BECecTfNHdb?si=c7a48826fb6e4712). enka is an interesting genre because of its ability to stem from many popular genres while maintaining its soul (kobushi vocals and japanese scales). this also makes me wonder how much of its genre design was purely commercial, adapting trending western genres to seek appeal from the japanese audience while maintaining both the genre itself and profits, but the commercialization of music is a long and multi-faceted debate that i’d want to skip to spend more time on music itself. another interesting characteristic that enka has is how the melody progressions sound really similar to those in genres like older cantopop and k-ballads, which makes sense since china and korea are both regionally and culturally near japan (no shit sherlock). overall, enka is a genre that maintains a traditional japanese flair while adapting to the globalizing trends of music at that time. it’s a key part of the general “kayokyokyu” umbrella of japanese music and the iconic kobushi vocal style have definitely influenced a lot of later japanese and asian music in general. next week we look at the bigger kayokyoku genre, which is something i’ve listened to for a long time now, but it’s time to go deeper!

## stylistics

- “kobushi” vocal style (essentially long vibrato vocalizations)  

- japanese scales and sometimes instrumentation (like the shamisen)  

- adaptable to many different popular genres like rock, jazz-pop, and easy listening (jus slap in those kobushi vocals and we chillin)
